HINDUSTAN PREMIER LEAGUE 1.0 · K. S. MOORTHY HALL

The Making of Hindustan Premier League 1.0

An idea my seniors brought home, a team of juniors behind the scenes, and two days that became a memory of their own.

OCTOBER 16 – 17, 2025 · 6 MIN READ

Some stories begin with a plan. This one began with an experience.

My seniors had visited the sister Arts and Science campus for a competition. While they were there, they came across an event that was quite different from the usual college activities — a live cricket auction. They got to participate in it themselves, and the experience fascinated them. The bidding, the excitement, the strategies, the competition between teams — it was something they genuinely enjoyed.

And somewhere along the way, a thought must have crossed their minds: Why don't we have something like this at our campus?

They returned with more than just memories of the competition. They brought back an idea.

01. An Idea That Travelled Back with My Seniors 💡

The thought was to recreate that experience at HITS, but this time, for the First year juniors. My seniors wanted them to enjoy the same excitement they had experienced at the sister campus. It would be an opportunity to bring students together, introduce them to something different, and, of course, add a little Mathematics Club flavour to the whole affair.

After all, what is a cricket auction without numbers? Player statistics, averages, strike rates, budgets, probabilities, and valuations — mathematics was already hiding in the game. We just had to bring it out.

That was how Hindustan Premier League 1.0 began taking shape.

My seniors led the initiative, with Chanti and the senior team at the forefront. We, the juniors, joined them in bringing the idea to life. There was plenty to figure out, and everyone had a part to play. The plan was to have teams of five, conduct a preliminary round, and bring the best teams together for a grand auction.

We imagined around forty teams participating. That already sounded like a sizeable event, and we were quite happy with the thought of getting that many students involved.

Little did we know what was waiting for us.

At that point, though, there was only excitement. We had an idea worth pursuing, seniors who were determined to make it happen, and a group of juniors ready to help.

The rest would have to be figured out along the way.

02. The Little World Behind HPL 🎬

There is a version of every college event that the audience gets to see — the posters, the photographs, the presentations, the final programme, and all the excitement on the day.

Then there is the other version.

The one involving endless discussions, spreadsheets, unfinished presentations, last-minute changes, and people trying to make sure everything works!

That was the world we found ourselves in while preparing HPL.

My seniors were leading the planning, and we juniors were involved in getting the different pieces ready. One of the biggest tasks was putting together the player database. An auction needed more than just a list of cricketers. We had to prepare player profiles, collect statistics, assign ratings and base prices, and organise everything so that the teams could make their decisions during the auction.

There was quite a bit of work behind those player slides. Every name needed its information, every player needed to be presented properly, and the PowerPoint had to be ready for the big day.

Alongside all this, we had to make sure the First year juniors knew what was coming.

And that meant promotions!

A few of my CSE friends helped us with the filming, and we started working on promotional videos. We experimented with different shots and ideas, trying to make HPL look as exciting as we imagined it would be. We eventually made five teasers, each with its own little attempt at building anticipation.

The videos were part of the fun of preparing for the event. Unfortunately, some of the music used in the other teasers was copyrighted. That doesn't mean the videos weren't shared with the juniors; it simply means I cannot publicly display those other videos on my website now. Copyright has its own rules, even when the memories are ours!

We also went around classrooms, spoke to students, and encouraged them to register. It was nice to see the idea gradually move beyond the organising team. Something my seniors had experienced elsewhere was now becoming an event that the First year juniors could look forward to at HITS.

Of course, putting together an event with a group of people is never completely free of disagreements. We had our discussions, different opinions, and moments when we had to rethink things. Sometimes we agreed quickly; sometimes we needed a little more convincing. But that was part of working together.

Somewhere between the player database, the promotional videos, the classroom visits, and all those planning conversations, HPL was slowly taking shape.

We were expecting around forty teams.

The registrations, however, had a surprise of their own.

03. Forty Was the Plan. Eighty Was the Reality. 📝

Thursday, 16 October 2025

When we imagined HPL, we had around forty teams in mind. It seemed like a good number — enough participation to make the event exciting, without making the arrangements too difficult.

But apparently, the First year juniors had other plans.

More than eighty teams registered!

We had expected forty, and suddenly we were looking at more than double that number. What had begun as a fairly ambitious event had grown into something much bigger. With two representatives from each team participating in the preliminary round, well over 160 students were now part of the qualifier.

It was one of those moments when you look at the numbers and wonder whether you have calculated something incorrectly!

But there was no time to be surprised for too long. The preliminary round had to be organised, and all those registrations had to be accommodated. Our faculty mentors supported us through the arrangements, and the team got ready for the first big day.

Thursday, 16 October 2025, finally arrived.

There is a particular feeling when something you've been discussing and preparing for weeks finally happens. Until then, everything exists in plans, documents, and conversations. Suddenly, there are actual participants, actual questions, and an event unfolding in front of you.

The preliminary round was underway.

For the First year juniors, this was their chance to make it to the auction. For us, it was the first real test of all the preparation that had gone into HPL.

And even after the quiz ended, the work wasn't over. We had to evaluate the responses, tally the scores, and identify the ten teams that would qualify for the grand auction. The evening went into getting the results ready and making sure the final list was in place.

Eventually, the ten finalists were decided.

More than eighty teams had started the journey, but only ten would make it to the auction.

The next day was going to be the one everyone had been waiting for.

04. Two Days, One Auction, and a Hall Full of Memories 🏟️

Friday, 17 October 2025

If Thursday was about finding the finalists, Friday was about giving them their stage.

K. S. Moorthy Hall was where HPL 1.0 would reach its biggest moment. The ten shortlisted teams were ready, the player database was prepared, and the auction was finally about to begin.

We wanted the experience to feel like a proper cricket auction. Every team had a budget to manage, players to choose, and a squad to build. It wasn't simply about picking the most famous cricketers. Teams had to think about how much they were willing to spend, which players they needed, and whether their choices would leave them with enough money for the rest of the squad.

The moment the bidding started, the atmosphere changed.

Names appeared, teams discussed their options, and bids began going up. Some teams were quick to make their moves, while others seemed to be calculating every decision. There were moments when a team really wanted a player but had to think twice about the price. And, of course, there was always the possibility that another team would outbid them!

It was lovely to see the auction come alive, especially knowing that this was the very experience my seniors had wanted to bring back for the First year juniors.

There were also a few moments that made us nervous. The Dean of Student Affairs and the Dean of Academics walked into the hall while the auction was underway. With the atmosphere as lively as it was, we naturally wondered how things would go. Thankfully, they appreciated the event and allowed us to continue.

And then there were the technical glitches, the little mistakes, and the unexpected situations that came with conducting a live event.

Now, there are certain things about 16 and 17 October that I would rather not explain in a public story. We made some errors, and there were moments that only the people who were actually there would fully understand. Those are the kinds of memories that make no sense to someone reading about the event years later, but can still make the people who experienced them smile.

I would love to sit with those people someday and talk about those moments again. Not because everything went perfectly, but precisely because it didn't.

Some of those mistakes have become cherished memories.

And perhaps that is the beauty of organising something for the first time. You prepare as much as you can, but the actual experience always gives you a few stories that no amount of planning could have predicted.

Through all of it, the auction continued. The teams completed their squads, the bidding came to an end, and the winners were announced.

HPL 1.0 had officially happened.

05. The Story Behind the Auction ❤️

Looking back, I think the best part of HPL 1.0 was the way the idea travelled from one batch to another.

My seniors went to the sister campus, experienced an event they genuinely enjoyed, and came back wanting to recreate that excitement at HITS. They took the lead, brought the idea forward, and gave us juniors the opportunity to help make it happen.

And the people who ultimately got to experience it were the First year juniors.

It was a lovely little chain of events: my seniors experienced something, wanted to share it, we juniors helped organise it, and a new batch got to create its own memories.

There was plenty happening behind the scenes — the player database, the presentations, the promotional videos, the registrations, the quiz, the auction, and all the little things that never make it into an event photograph.

But those are the things I remember.

I remember the excitement of seeing the registrations cross what we had imagined. I remember the work that went into preparing the auction. I remember the people who helped us, the discussions we had, and those two days that gave us plenty to laugh about afterwards.

HPL 1.0 wasn't a perfectly executed event, and I don't think it needs to be remembered that way. It was something my seniors wanted to bring to our campus, something we juniors helped put together, and something the First year juniors got to experience.

It was a beginning. And for those of us who were part of it, it was much more than just a cricket auction.

Some stories are best told through photographs. Others are best told over a conversation with the people who were there.

HPL 1.0 belongs to both.

And above all, thank You, Jesus, for Your grace throughout the journey. ✝️❤️
