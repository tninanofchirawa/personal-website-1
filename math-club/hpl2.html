AUGUST – NOVEMBER 2026 · MATHEMATICS CLUB

Hindustan Premier League 2.0

Calculated Chaos, A Little Humility, and One More Day to Remember

2 NOVEMBER 2026 · 6 MIN READ

The dust had barely settled on HPL 1.0 when we started thinking about what came next.

01. Learning from the First Innings 🏏

Our first edition had been quite an experience. The hall was packed, the bidding was intense, and the first-years had thoroughly enjoyed the auction. From the outside, it looked like a wonderful success. But those of us who had been running around behind the scenes knew there was another side to the story.

There had been last-minute calculations, a fair share of confusion, and moments when things could easily have gone wrong. We had managed to pull it off, but we knew we could do better.

So, during August and September, we sat down and went through what had happened. We discussed the mistakes, looked at what could be improved, and began rethinking how the next edition should work.

We wanted HPL 2.0 to be more organised, more engaging, and, most importantly, more mathematical.

The auction was the heart of the event, but we wanted the mathematics to be more than just a name attached to it. Cricket itself offered plenty of opportunities: run rates, strike rates, percentages, probability and a whole lot of number crunching.

There was quite a bit of brainstorming, a few questionable ideas, and plenty of enthusiasm from our side.

And then, somewhere along the way, we came up with what we thought was a brilliant idea.

02. The App That Never Made It!

We had spent so much time thinking about the problems in HPL 1.0 that we became convinced we could solve almost all of them with technology.

Why calculate team budgets manually when a computer could do it? Why update spreadsheets when everything could happen automatically? Why not build an entire live-bidding application?

And so, we decided to build one from scratch.

For nearly the whole of September, we were caught up in this little adventure. We worked with our CSE seniors, consulted people, designed player cards, wrote bidding logic and tried to make the whole system work.

From our perspective, it was brilliant. We had a working application, and we were quite excited to show it off.

Then came the day we presented it to our seniors.

Chanti and Dhilip watched our demonstration, considered what we had built, and brought us back to reality.

Their point was simple: HPL was an offline auction. Why bring everyone into K. S. Moorthy Hall only to have them sit in front of screens and play through an application?

And honestly, they had a point.

We had become so occupied with building something technically impressive that we had almost forgotten what made the auction enjoyable in the first place. The excitement of raising a paddle, the hesitation before placing a bid, the arguments between teammates and the noise of an entire hall reacting to a single player — none of that could quite be recreated by a screen.

There was also the small matter of getting a hundred-plus students connected to a system over the hall's Wi-Fi without something eventually going wrong!

Well, that was the end of our grand application.

We put the idea aside and returned to our slides, spreadsheets and calculators. It was a little disappointing after all the effort, but looking back, it was probably one of the better decisions we made.

Sometimes, a good idea isn't necessarily the one with the most technology behind it.

03. Back to the Drawing Board

With the application out of the picture, we started putting together the actual event materials.

This time, we wanted the preliminary round to have a stronger mathematical flavour. After all, we were organising this through the Mathematics Club, and it would be a little strange if the mathematics took a back seat!

We worked on the question paper, the player ratings, the auction arrangements and the rules. There were several things to balance, and we spent quite some time trying to get everything in place.

Our seniors played an important role during this phase. They guided us, helped us think through the arrangements and pointed out things we might otherwise have overlooked. Chanti and Dhilip, in particular, helped keep our plans grounded.

We juniors had our share of ideas — some useful, some perhaps a little too ambitious — but the preparation was a good learning experience.

One thing was certain: HPL 2.0 had to be better than the first edition.

04. Fifty-Three Teams and a Day of Calculated Chaos

Monday, 2 November 2026

There was just one little complication.

We had to conduct the entire event on the same day.

The reason was that 30 October had already been set aside for another Mathematics Club activity, our Mathematics in AI poster presentation. That deserves a story of its own, so I'll save it for another time.

With the schedule getting rather crowded, we settled on 2 November 2026 for HPL 2.0.

And that meant we had quite a bit to get through in a single day.

The final preparations involved revisiting some of the rules. A few changes were necessary before the preliminary test, and we had to be careful about how we communicated them. We didn't want participants arriving with one understanding of the rules only to discover something different on the day. So, we worked through the changes and made sure everything was properly clarified.

The question paper, which had already been prepared, was also designed to be more mathematics-oriented than before. We wanted the participants to think, calculate and apply a little mathematics rather than simply answer questions about cricket.

When the day finally arrived, we had another surprise waiting for us.

Fifty-three teams, all from the first year, turned up to participate!

With several activities happening around campus, and some of our juniors having to attend training camps, there was plenty going on at the same time. But we got ourselves organised and began moving things forward.

During the preparation, our seniors had taken more of the lead, guiding us through the process. Now, it was our turn to take charge.

There were papers to distribute, participants to coordinate, answers to collect and scores to work through. With fifty-three teams involved, it certainly wasn't a small task.

But somehow, things came together.

The preliminary round went ahead, and after all the calculations and checking, we had our finalists. Ten teams made it through to the auction.

And just like that, we were ready for the part everyone had been waiting for.

05. The Auction Begins! 🔨

With the preliminary round behind us, K. S. Moorthy Hall was ready for its transformation.

We had our presentations in place, the spreadsheets connected and the player details ready to go. The auction was no longer just an idea we had been discussing for weeks. It was happening right in front of us.

As the first players appeared on the screen, the atmosphere began to change.

Paddles went up, bids increased, and teams started keeping a close eye on their remaining budgets. Some were determined to get their favourite players, while others were carefully saving their money for the rest of their squad.

There were moments of excitement, a few unexpected bids and plenty of calculations happening around the room.

Our original plan had been to automate everything through the application, but here we were, running the auction with spreadsheets and presentations. And, surprisingly enough, it worked!

We had to keep track of the bids, update the teams' budgets and make sure the squad requirements were followed. It required attention, coordination and quite a bit of patience.

But the hall had the atmosphere we had hoped for. People were involved, teams were making decisions, and every new player brought another round of excitement.

It was calculated chaos, just as we had imagined.

06. The Final Whistle

Eventually, the last bid was made, the final squads were completed, and HPL 2.0 came to an end.

Looking back, the journey was just as interesting as the event itself.

We had started with the lessons of HPL 1.0, spent weeks trying to build an application, watched that idea get scrapped, returned to our spreadsheets, made our preparations and finally brought fifty-three teams together for the preliminary round.

There were things that went according to plan and things that didn't. But that was part of the experience.

I'm particularly grateful to our seniors, who guided us during the preparation and helped us approach the event with a little more confidence. And to the juniors who worked behind the scenes and helped bring everything together — it was a team effort.

More than anything, HPL 2.0 taught me that organising an event isn't simply about having a great idea. It is about adapting when things don't work, trusting the people around you and making the best of what you have.

We didn't end up using our fancy application. We didn't have everything perfectly under control. But we did have a hall full of enthusiastic students, an auction worth remembering and a team that managed to pull it all together.

And perhaps that was enough.

HPL 1.0 gave us the experience. HPL 2.0 gave us the confidence.

As for HPL 3.0? Well, let's see what we come up with next!

Above all, thank You, Jesus. For the opportunity, the people and the journey. ✝️❤️
