THE TAMIL NADU STATE MATHS TECH INNOVATION SUMMIT · 2025

The Bottle That Went to the State Summit

One water bottle, a district win, a state award and a lot of mathematics.

{{ page.story_period }} · {{ page.reading_time }}

01. "Pack Your Bags. We're Building a Project."

It all started in January 2025, before I even knew what I was getting myself into.

My seniors came up to us juniors and essentially told us to pack our bags and get ready. We had a project to make. A mathematics project.

I was genuinely confused. What on earth was I supposed to make? A mathematics project? What could I possibly build with the mathematics I knew?

My seniors reassured us that they would help. They even had something ready: a project about mathematicians, their contributions and the Indian mathematical minds who had shaped the subject through their discoveries.

It sounded pretty good to me. A project about the people who built mathematics? I thought we had a decent starting point.

But there was another project happening at the other end of the room. And that one involved a bottle.

02. A Poster, a Lecture and a Little Déjà Vu

Before getting to the bottle, there's a small flashback worth mentioning. My seniors had already been through a poster presentation at Mathophilia 2024, organised by the Department of Mathematics and the Hindustan Mathematics Club on 23 February 2024.

They had prepared a poster about mathematicians and their contributions. Later, when it was our turn to prepare for a mathematics project, they handed us that very same poster.

And when I say handed it to us, I mean it quite literally. It was almost like a book — the same material, no major changes, and a very practical instruction: make do with it!

There was a small catch, though. We were going to compete against the very seniors who had originally prepared it.

At Mathophilia, my teammate and I were presenting as a pair. My teammate was much more comfortable explaining the poster, so at one point he told me to head to the lecture hall and attend the lecture. He could handle the explanation.

So I went. And, rather impressively, I managed to miss my own team's presentation. We eventually lost to our seniors — the original creators of the poster. Fair enough, I suppose. We were competing against the people who had made the thing!

That was the poster story. But the bottle had a much longer history.

03. Before the Bottle Became a Project

The water rocket wasn't a last-minute idea invented for the January 2025 competition. My seniors had first made it back in February 2024.

They took their first version to the university's National Science Day / SDG event on 28 February 2024, where it was seen by the Physics and Chemistry departments.

This was a much earlier version of the project. It wasn't the same design, appearance or trajectory that we would hear about later. At that stage, the goal was much simpler.

Could they make it fly?

And when it did, that was enough to make the experiment worthwhile. The calculations, charts and more developed explanations would come later.

The first version: no fancy photo album, no elaborate display and no documented launch photographs to show here. This part of the story comes from what I learnt from my seniors.

04. Meanwhile, There Was a Water Bottle...

Back in January 2025, while we were looking through our mathematicians and their contributions, I noticed what my seniors were working on.

Something involving water. And a rocket.

Well, to be honest, it looked rather silly.

It was a water rocket made using a reusable plastic bottle, water and pressurised air from an air pump. I couldn't quite understand how that was supposed to compete with the elaborate mathematics projects other colleges might bring.

I told my senior what I thought. He just smiled.

Then came a piece of advice that I still remember:

"People don't necessarily give awards to the most complicated project. They remember the one that catches their attention. It's all about how you sell your project."

He explained that plenty of teams would arrive with books, charts, elaborate models and pages of calculations. But if we wanted to stand out, we had to make people interested in what we had built. Use mathematics, explain the idea and make them want to see it.

I listened, although I wasn't entirely convinced. Then I asked him what exactly he was planning to build.

"A water rocket."

I looked at him. He looked at me. And then came the line: "When it flies, everything is good."

Fair enough. 😂

What I didn't fully appreciate at the time was that there was quite a bit of mathematics behind that bottle. By 2025, the project had developed beyond simply getting it to fly. There were calculations, optimisation work, charts and technical explanations supporting the design.

The bottle was simple. The work behind it wasn't.

05. January 9: The District-Level Competition

On 9 January 2025, my seniors headed to St. Joseph's College of Engineering for the district-level Mathematics Project Competition.

My classmates and I weren't exactly expecting to win anything. We had made our peace with that possibility. In fact, I think we had mentally prepared ourselves for a much simpler mission: support our seniors, get our On-Duty attendance and enjoy the day.

The event also had academic lectures and sessions conducted by professors and researchers. I was sent along with some classmates to attend one of them.

It turned out to be quite interesting. We were introduced to mathematical ideas such as graph colouring and connections, and how these ideas could extend into practical applications involving networks and satellites.

There was just one small problem: the project competition was happening elsewhere. I wasn't there to watch my seniors present the rocket or see the judging.

I was attending lectures while the actual rocket business was happening outside.

Later, I heard from my classmates and seniors that the rocket had flown approximately 25 feet. The judges had apparently enjoyed the demonstration so much that they wanted to try it themselves, and it was repeated three times.

I didn't witness any of that myself, but the result was clear: my seniors had won the district-level competition!

That little bottle had earned its place in the next round.

06. February 14: The State-Level Recognition

The district-level win wasn't the end of the journey. The project went on to the Tamil Nadu State Maths Tech Innovation Summit 2025, held at Queen Mary's College, Chennai, on 14 February 2025.

This time, the recognition was at the state level. My seniors, A. Kousalya and M. Dhilip, received the Best Project Award for their project, Application of Mathematics in Water Rocket.

The award was part of the summit conducted by the National Council for Science and Technology Communication. It was quite something to see a project that had started with a simple question — could we make a bottle fly? — receive recognition at a state-level event.

The Department of Mathematics at HITS also recorded their achievement on its official website.

07. The Part You Won't Find in the Photographs

One thing worth saying: most of the photographs from these events show the team, the certificates and the awards. They don't show the actual water rocket demonstration.

There is one photograph with the project display, charts and medals together. The rocket is visible off to the side, but the photograph is really about the medals and the recognition.

So, while the pictures preserve the moments around the project, the story of the rocket itself has to be told through the people who built it and the work they put into it.

Looking back, I think that's what makes this project interesting. It began with a simple bottle that needed to fly. By the time it reached the state summit, there was a lot more mathematics, thought and explanation behind it.

A LITTLE LESSON FROM A WATER BOTTLE

Sometimes, Simple Ideas Go a Long Way.

I initially thought a water bottle would never stand a chance against all those elaborate models and complicated-looking projects. My seniors proved otherwise.

The mathematics mattered. The calculations mattered. But so did the courage to build something, make it work and explain it well.

08. The Story Wasn't Over Yet...

February wasn't quite finished with the water rocket. There was another SDG event on 28 February 2025, with another chapter of its own.

But that was a separate event, with a separate story. And it deserves a page of its own.

So, as always... here's the next chapter.
