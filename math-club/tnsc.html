THE TAMIL NADU STATE CHAMPIONSHIP · 2025

The Bottle That Went to the State Championships

FEBRUARY 2024 – JANUARY 2025 · ONE WATER BOTTLE, TWO VICTORIES & A LOT OF MATHEMATICS

01. "Pack Your Bags. We're Building a Project."

It all started before I even knew what was happening.

January 2025. My seniors came up to us juniors and essentially told us to pack our bags and get ready. We had a project to make. A mathematics project.

I was genuinely shocked. What on earth was I supposed to make? A mathematics project? What could I possibly build with the mathematics I knew?

My seniors reassured us that they would help us out this time. They even had something ready for us: a project they had previously developed, detailing great mathematicians, their contributions and the Indian mathematical minds who had shaped the subject through their discoveries and inventions.

Honestly, I thought it was awesome. A project about the people who built mathematics? That sounded like a pretty good deal.

Little did I know that the real show was happening at the other end of the room.

02. Meanwhile, There Was a Water Bottle...

While we were looking through our mathematicians and their contributions, I noticed what my seniors were working on.

Something involving water. And a rocket.

Well, to be honest, it looked rather silly.

They were planning to use a project they had originally developed during their first year for a college-level Mathematics competition. It was a water rocket, made using a reusable plastic bottle, filled with water and powered by pressurised air from an air pump.

I couldn't quite understand how that was supposed to compete with all the elaborate mathematics projects other colleges would bring. I told my senior what I thought.

He just smiled.

Then came a piece of advice that I still remember: "People don't necessarily give awards to the most complicated project. They remember the one that catches their attention. It's all about how you sell your project."

He explained that plenty of teams would arrive with books, charts, elaborate models and pages of calculations. But if we wanted to stand out, we had to make people interested in what we had built. Use mathematics, sell the idea and make them want to see it.

I listened, although I wasn't entirely convinced. Then I asked him what exactly he was planning to build.

"A water rocket."

I looked at him. He looked at me. And then he said something along the lines of: "When it flies, everything is good."

Fair enough. 😂

What I didn't fully appreciate at the time was that there was actually quite a bit of mathematics behind that bottle. My seniors had pages of calculations, optimisation work, charts and other material supporting the design. They had done their homework. They simply didn't need to put all of it in front of the judges to make their point.

03. A Bottle with a History

As I later came to discover, this wasn't the first time that little water rocket had made an appearance.

Back in February 2024, on National Science Day, my seniors had presented the same project at the university's SDG competition when they were first-year Math students. And they had WON!

It was the first time the Math seniors had won a competition with their bottle rocket, beating the first-year B.Tech teams by wowing the judges with its flight, backed by calculations, charts and solid explanations. So, this bottle already had a winning story before I even started questioning its potential!

I learnt this part of the story later, when my seniors were recounting their experiences to the HOD after returning from the district competition. Suddenly, the project made a little more sense. This wasn't some last-minute experiment they had put together. They had built it, tested it, presented it and already seen it succeed.

And now, in January 2025, they were taking it back out for another competition.

04. January 9: A Day of Mathematics, Rockets and Biryani

On January 9, we headed to St. Joseph's College of Engineering for the district-level Mathematics Project Competition.

My classmates and I weren't exactly expecting to win anything. We had already made our peace with that possibility. In fact, I think we had mentally prepared ourselves for a much simpler mission: support our seniors, get our On-Duty attendance and enjoy the day. There was free food too. So, naturally, morale was reasonably high. 😂

The event wasn't just about the project competition, though. Alongside it, there were academic lectures and sessions conducted by professors and researchers from reputed institutions, including the University of Madras and other research establishments.

I was sent along with some of my classmates to attend one of these sessions. It turned out to be quite interesting! We were introduced to topics such as graph colouring, connections and how these mathematical ideas extended into practical applications. The discussions eventually touched upon things like satellites and the kinds of networks and connections involved in such systems.

It was a rather different side of mathematics from what we usually encountered in our classrooms. But, of course, there was one small problem. The water rocket competition was happening elsewhere, and I had absolutely no idea what was going on!

I couldn't watch my seniors present their project or see what happened during the judging. I was attending the lectures while the actual rocket business was happening outside.

05. Biryani, Confidence and the Results

After the lectures, before the results were announced, we all went together as a group to enjoy the food. And when I say food, I mean biryani and all the other goodies that came with the day. It was a pretty decent On-Duty outing!

That was when my seniors started telling us how things had gone. Apparently, they had a very strong chance of winning. They were talking about how enthusiastic the judges had been about their project, how interested they were in the demonstration and how much attention the rocket had attracted.

I was listening, but I still wasn't entirely sure what to expect.

I had seen some of the other projects around the venue. One particularly impressive model from another reputed college had caught my attention. It was a fairly large wooden mechanism, something like a roller, designed to demonstrate probability by showing when a ball would fall. It looked expensive, well-built and technically impressive.

And then there was our water bottle. I couldn't help wondering how the two would compare.

By evening, the results were out.

And guess what? My seniors had WON!

According to my classmates who witnessed the demonstration, the rocket shot approximately 25 feet into the air. The judges apparently enjoyed it so much that they wanted to try it themselves, and the demonstration was repeated three times!

Three times!

Suddenly, my senior's advice made a lot more sense. He hadn't needed to overwhelm the judges with pages of calculations. He had built something that demonstrated its idea right in front of them.

The mathematics was behind the design, the calculations and the optimisation. The charts and supporting work were there to explain it. But the flight was what captured everyone's attention. That little reusable bottle had done exactly what he had promised.

It flew. And just like that, the project had won at the district level and qualified for the Tamil Nadu State Mathematics Project Championship.

06. January 20: The State Championship

The story didn't end at St. Joseph's.

On January 20, my seniors travelled to Stella Maris College to compete at the Tamil Nadu State Mathematics Project Championship, alongside the winners from other districts.

I couldn't attend this one either, which is something I still wish I had been there for.

But the news that came back was wonderful. The same project that had impressed the judges at the district competition managed to capture their attention at the State level too.

They WON THE STATE CHAMPIONSHIP!

I was genuinely thrilled for them. Watching an idea that I had initially dismissed as a rather silly water-bottle experiment go all the way to a State Championship victory was quite something.

And perhaps, it taught me a lesson I wouldn't have learnt from a textbook.

Sometimes, the best project isn't the one with the most complicated-looking model or the thickest explanation. Sometimes, it's the one that makes someone stop, look and say: "Wait. How did you do that?"

My seniors had understood something I hadn't. The mathematics mattered, but so did the way you communicated it. They had the calculations, the optimisation and the technical work to back up their idea. They just knew when to let the rocket do the talking.

07. The Story Wasn't Over Yet...

Of course, my seniors weren't done with the water rocket. After the State Championship, they started thinking about how they could improve the project further, make a few modifications and take it in a new direction.

There was another competition coming up in February: the university-level SDG competition.

And apparently, our little water rocket had another flight left in it.

But that... is a story for another page.
