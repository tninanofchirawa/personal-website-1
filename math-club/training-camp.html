THE ST. JOSEPH'S PREP · 2025

The Making of a Math Club Team

MAY – AUGUST 2025 · SIX DAYS, SEVEN STUDENTS & A WHOLE LOT OF MATHEMATICS

01. A Legacy to Continue

It all started in May 2025. Well, the story actually goes a little further back.

On January 9 and 10, 2025, my Mathematics seniors participated in the district-level Mathematics Project Competition at St. Joseph's College of Engineering. Their project? A water rocket.

No fancy programming, no complicated electronics. Just mathematics, basic optimisation, calculations, calculators and online graphing tools. They worked on the design, experimented with weight and tried to figure out how to get the best possible performance. And somehow, they managed to turn a pressurised bottle filled with water into something that shot into the sky like a missile!

They won at the district level and progressed to the Tamil Nadu State Mathematics Project Championship at Stella Maris College. And they WON!

I wasn't part of the project team back then. I was there to support them at the district-level competition, watching my seniors turn what seemed like a simple idea into something that went on to win at the State Championships. Little did I know that this project would come up again in the months that followed. The seniors would later build upon that work for the university-level SDG competition, where I would get the opportunity to join the team myself. But that's a story for another page.

By May, the HOD had been observing the developments within our Mathematics department and the work our seniors had been doing. She called me in with an unexpected proposition: she wanted me to become the next President of the Mathematics Club.

Naturally, I had one question: "If my seniors have already done all this, why me?" Turns out, my seniors had actually recommended me for the position. Even better, they were willing to support me along the way. That meant a lot. Thank you, seniors!

02. A New Team, A New Direction

I was officially going to be the President, but I never really saw it as a one-person job. My seniors and I worked as a team. Sometimes I led, sometimes they did. Depending on what needed to be done, we simply switched roles. The designation might have been mine, but the support system was very much ours.

The HOD had another expectation, though: new events, new projects and a more active Mathematics Club. Our usual annual outing was the St. Joseph's Mathematics Competition. It was always fun to attend, but we generally went as participants, competing alongside other Mathematics students. This time, we wanted to do more.

My seniors had a good laugh when I agreed to help build the new Math Club team. They knew I already had mountains of work waiting for me. In hindsight, they probably knew exactly what I was getting myself into.

The Math Club induction day finally arrived. The first-year batch of 2025–29 was there, and I was officially inducted as President. We also announced something new: Mathematics training classes for the juniors.

You see, somewhere between finishing their +2 examinations and entering college, most students seem to undergo a mysterious memory reset. The entire mathematics syllabus gets wiped from the brain, and the remaining knowledge is apparently sent straight to the recycling bin. Our mission? Recover that lost memory chip.

There was just one tiny problem: they had their internal examinations coming up. And I, in my infinite wisdom, decided that daily voluntary mathematics classes would be a brilliant idea. Rookie mistake.

03. Twenty Became Seven. Seven Stayed.

The first day started with around 20 students. We began with notes, worked through questions, practised concepts and gradually built up the material. It was a new experience for most of us. We weren't just preparing for a competition; we were also figuring out how to organise a proper training programme along the way.

Then the numbers started dropping. Twenty became fifteen, and fifteen became seven. But those seven stayed.

And honestly, that deserves a shout-out. These students were juggling internal examinations, additional Mathematics classes, hackathons and other college activities. Yet they kept showing up and putting in the effort. The remaining eight students from the original group chose to prepare through self-study instead. Everyone was finding their own way to prepare, and both efforts deserve recognition.

There was quite a bit of learning happening behind the scenes too. Preparing notes, designing questions and, especially, setting tests were new experiences for my classmates and me. My seniors, who already knew how these things worked, stepped in to guide us, review our preparation and provide an overall direction. They helped us understand what needed to be done while giving us room to handle the actual work.

Another new responsibility for me was preparing progress reports every three days. This wasn't simply about recording attendance or test scores. I had to look at how the students were progressing, identify where we needed to improve, make changes to the training and present the updates to the HOD and the Mathematics Club Head Teacher. Every three days meant another round of analysing, reporting and adjusting. It was my first time handling a cycle like that, and I learnt quite a bit along the way.

And then came the scheduling challenge. We had just six days before the event to get everything done. Six days to organise sessions, prepare material, conduct tests, review progress and get the team ready.

In those six days, we attempted to cover almost the entire Class 12 Mathematics syllabus, along with a substantial portion of Class 11. We prepared, practised, tested ourselves and kept making adjustments wherever necessary. The students put in the effort, my seniors kept us on track, and I was learning how to bring all the moving pieces together.

It was exhausting. It was ambitious. And perhaps, looking back, a little unreasonable. But nobody decided to call it quits.

📸 Undoing the post-+2 memory wipe, one daily session at a time

04. The Day of the Competition

Finally, the day arrived. Around 20 students represented HITS at the St. Joseph's Mathematics Competition. Our contingent included five seniors and four juniors from the Mathematics Club (including me), alongside eleven students from the training group. It was quite a turnout!

And we weren't exactly walking into a small competition. We were up against some of the big names: Loyola, Madras Christian College (MCC), Rajalakshmi, Sri Sairam, Sathyabama, University of Madras, Panimalar, Women's Christian College (WCC) and Ethiraj, among others. These were established institutions bringing teams with plenty of experience. Suddenly, our little six-day preparation camp felt even smaller!

There was another side to all this preparation. My classmates and I were also figuring out how to put together tests and question papers. None of us had really done this kind of work before. We had to come up with questions, make sure the answers were correct, check that every question was actually solvable and solve the entire set ourselves — all without taking forever. It was new territory, and there was a strange pressure to get good at it almost immediately. We were learning on the job, but we also wanted to get it right. Exciting, but honestly, a little scary too.

Coming back to the competition, the first rounds were where things got interesting. Most of us juniors struggled to get past them. After all the preparation, the notes, the tests and the effort we had put in, it was a little disappointing to see our team get stopped so early.

Meanwhile, my seniors were having the time of their lives! They had put in comparatively little preparation for this particular competition, but they simply walked in, breezed through the initial rounds and kept making it to the final stages of the events they had entered. Apparently, experience does count for something!

And the funniest part? They had pretty much come to enjoy the day — and make the most of the free biryani and ice cream! While we juniors were busy wondering where all our preparation had gone, the seniors were having an absolute blast. I suppose they had earned the right to enjoy themselves!

But jokes aside, watching them do so well was genuinely fun. There was a little disappointment on our side, especially after the amount of work we had put in, but seeing our seniors represent the same Mathematics Club and make it to those final rounds made me happy. Different batches, different teams, different levels of preparation — but we were all there as one Math Club.

Looking back, I am grateful to everyone who made this happen. To my seniors: thank you for trusting me enough to recommend me, for standing beside me and for helping me figure things out along the way. To my classmates: thank you for jumping into unfamiliar work and helping me put together the tests and preparation material under pressure. And to the students who attended the training sessions, especially the seven who stayed: thank you for showing up and putting in the effort.

We may not have had the results we hoped for that day, but we had built something new. We had organised, prepared, tested, learnt and shown up — all in a very short time. For a team that was just getting started, that was a pretty good beginning.

Of course, even the seniors weren't winning every final. Whenever they lost one, they had a saying: "We lost the battle, but the war is still on!" And off they went to the next event, ready for another round.

But the real highlight came when we finally sat down for the biryani. One of my seniors looked at me, pointed at the plate and said: "Timothy, we lost the battle... but look at this. We have won the war!"

And honestly, with a plate of biryani and free ice cream waiting, who was I to argue? 😂
