THE ST. JOSEPH'S PREP · 2025

From "You Don't Belong There" to Running the Camp

HOW A SIMPLE PRAYER LED TO SIX DAYS OF CHALK DUST & HUMBLING REALITY

It all started in January, during my second semester, when I made a rather surprising discovery: our department actually had a Math Club, but none of us from M.Sc. Mathematics were part of it. Naturally, I went up to a faculty member to ask why mathematics students were being left out of their own club. The answer? "You don't belong there." Well, that was awkward. A little disappointed, I walked away, said a small prayer about it, and moved on.

Fast forward to May 2025, and apparently, God had other plans. Out of nowhere, the HOD called me in and appointed me President of the Mathematics Club. Just like that! With the momentum from our earlier science competitions and the support of our seniors and faculty, we finally had the opportunity to rebuild the club and give it a fresh start. Quite the plot twist.

One of the first things on our agenda was preparing for the annual St. Joseph's Math Competition. We had a new batch of students, and I wanted to make sure our department was represented. There was, however, one tiny obstacle: most students had practically factory-reset their mathematical brains after their +2 board exams. So, I volunteered to train them from scratch. My seniors and friends thought it was hilarious. Looking back, they probably knew exactly what I was getting myself into.

We started with roughly 150 students on the club's membership list, but only about 50 actually showed up. I proposed daily preparation sessions, and initially, 15 students committed to the idea. As the days went by, that number gradually shrank until we were left with a core team of just seven. Seven students, a mountain of mathematics, and a very ambitious training schedule.

The next few days were a proper challenge. We had to cover huge portions of mathematics in a ridiculously short time, all while making sure the students could keep up with their regular classes and internal examinations. Between explaining concepts, solving problems, revisiting basics, and trying to keep everyone motivated, those sessions were quite an experience. It also gave me a newfound respect for teachers. I love mathematics, but teaching it under a deadline? Let's just say I may have ruled out becoming a professor.

Eventually, competition day arrived. At St. Joseph's, we found ourselves competing against colleges that had brought entire M.Sc. squads, while our little team was still finding its feet. We gave it our best, but we didn't win. And yes, it stung a little. But looking back, the result isn't what I remember most. I remember those seven students showing up every day, studying despite their internals, putting up with my many flaws as a first-time trainer, and sticking with the preparation until the very end. We may not have brought home a trophy, but we certainly came away with a story worth remembering.

📸 Undoing the post-+2 memory wipe, one daily session at a time
LESSON LEARNED Six days, seven students, countless problems, and a whole lot of chalk dust. We didn't win the competition, but I learnt that building a team and helping people grow can be just as rewarding as bringing home a trophy. To those seven who stuck around till the end — thank you for making the chaos worthwhile.
