THE ST. JOSEPH'S PREP · 2025

The Making of a Math Club Team

Six days, seven students, three checkpoints, and a whole lot of mathematics.

MAY – AUGUST 2025 · 7 MIN READ

01. A Legacy to Continue 🏆

It all started in May 2025. Well, the story actually goes a little further back.

On January 9 and 10, 2025, my Mathematics seniors participated in the district-level Mathematics Project Competition at St. Joseph's College of Engineering. Their project? A water rocket.

No fancy programming. No complicated electronics. Just a plastic bottle, water, air pressure, mathematics, basic optimisation, calculations, calculators, and a few online graphing tools. They experimented with the design, played around with weight, and tried to figure out how to get the best possible performance out of something that, at first glance, looked like an ordinary soda bottle.

And somehow, they managed to turn it into something that shot into the sky like a missile! 🚀

They won at the district level and progressed to the Tamil Nadu State Mathematics Project Championship, held on 14 February 2025 at Queen Mary's College, Chennai. And they won there too, taking home the Best Project Award!

I wasn't part of the project team back then. I was there to support them at the district-level competition, watching my seniors turn what seemed like a simple idea into something that went on to win at the State Championships.

Little did I know that this project would come up again in the months that followed. The seniors would later build upon that work for the university-level SDG competition, where I would get the opportunity to join the team myself.

But that's a story for another page.

By May, our HOD had been observing the developments within the Mathematics Department and the work our seniors had been doing. She called me in with an unexpected proposition: she wanted me to become the next President of the Mathematics Club.

Naturally, I had one question:

"If my seniors have already done all this, why me?"

Turns out, my seniors had actually recommended me for the position. They saw something in me that I wasn't entirely sure I saw in myself yet. Even better, they promised they wouldn't simply disappear after handing over the designation. They would be around to guide me, help me figure things out, and make sure I didn't have to learn absolutely everything the hard way.

That meant a lot.

Thank you, seniors! ❤️

And just like that, I found myself holding a title, a responsibility, and absolutely no idea what the next few months had in store for me.

02. A New Team, A New Direction 📚

By May 2025, I was wrapping up my first year and stepping into my second. I was officially going to be the President, but I never really saw it as a one-person job.

My seniors and I worked as a team. Sometimes I led, sometimes they did; depending on what needed to be done, we simply switched roles. The designation might have been mine on paper, but the support system was very much ours.

Over the next couple of months, we kept things rolling. Between June and early August, there were the usual department events, orientations, and small activities that kept the Mathematics Club alive and moving.

Then came August, when the fresh batch of 2025–29 first-years arrived on campus.

Our HOD called me in with a clear target: Mathiesta 2025, the national-level mathematics symposium hosted by St. Joseph's Institute of Technology, OMR, scheduled for 30 August 2025. In previous years, students had gone as individual participants, but this time, the department wanted to field a coordinated team.

During the induction, I was introduced to the incoming juniors as the new Mathematics Club President, representing the M.Sc. Mathematics side, while B.Tech had its own representative.

I walked up on stage, pitched the competition, and announced our plan for training sessions. When I asked who was interested, around thirty out of fifty students raised their hands.

Thirty hands!

For a moment, I thought we had pulled off something brilliant. A hall full of keen first-years, an upcoming competition, and a training programme. What could possibly go wrong?

Well, somewhere between finishing their +2 board examinations and entering college, most first-years seem to undergo a mysterious mathematical memory reset.

Algebra? Gone.

Calculus? Somewhere in the mental recycling bin.

Trigonometry? Last seen before the board exams.

Our mission was to recover that lost memory chip. 🧠

There was just one tiny problem: the circular had reached us with only seven days to spare. With travel and logistics to account for, we had just six days to prepare, teach, practise, and get everyone ready.

And the juniors had their internal examinations coming up.

And I, in my infinite wisdom, decided that daily voluntary mathematics classes during internal exam week was a brilliant idea.

Rookie mistake. 😂

03. Twenty Became Seven. Seven Stayed. ⏳

With only six working days on the clock, we didn't have the luxury of a gentle warm-up.

On the first day, around twenty students showed up. We began with mental arithmetic, quick calculations, and the fundamentals before gradually moving into the mathematics they would need for the competition.

Then college life caught up.

Internal examinations, lab submissions, hackathons, assignments, and regular coursework were all happening at the same time. Attendance started dropping.

Twenty became fifteen.

Fifteen became seven.

But those seven stayed.

And honestly, that deserves a shout-out.

These students were juggling their own college responsibilities while attending additional mathematics classes. They kept showing up, working through questions, revisiting concepts, and putting in the effort even when things became hectic. Others continued preparing on their own, but these seven became the core group we worked with throughout the training sessions.

Meanwhile, my classmates and I had our own learning curve.

Preparing notes was one thing. Designing competition questions was another.

We had to come up with problems, make sure the mathematics was correct, and figure out whether they could realistically be solved within the allotted time. Then, of course, we had to solve them ourselves.

Thankfully, our seniors were there to review our work, guide us, and keep us from going completely off track.

We also conducted a few online tests to see how the students were progressing. Each checkpoint covered a different collection of topics, gradually moving from algebra and combinatorics to calculus, probability, statistics, and geometry.

It wasn't just about getting marks. We wanted to understand what they were comfortable with, what was giving them trouble, and where we needed to spend the little time we had left.

During a college break, we even sent them home with a 90-question practice packet. Because apparently, six days of mathematics wasn't enough. 😂

And somewhere in between classes, practice, and all the chaos, there was another responsibility: progress reports every few days. We had to keep our HOD and Math Club Head Teacher updated on how things were going and what we needed to work on next.

Teach. → Test. → Analyse. → Report. → Adjust. → Repeat.

It became our little cycle.

Just when we were getting into the rhythm, Dr. A. Rajkumar Sir dropped by to give the students a motivational talk and his blessings. It was a welcome boost as we entered the final stretch.

Looking back, trying to squeeze nearly two years of higher-secondary mathematics into six days, right in the middle of internal examinations, was probably a little unreasonable.

But nobody called it quits.

Those seven juniors kept grinding. My classmates kept helping. Our seniors kept us anchored. And somehow, by the end of Day Six, we had a team ready to head to St. Joseph's.

We hadn't magically restored every mathematical memory lost after +2.

But we had done what we could.

And it was time to see what happened.

04. The Day of the Competition 🏆

Match day finally arrived on Saturday, 30 August 2025.

Around twenty of us represented HITS at St. Joseph's Institute of Technology for Mathiesta 2025. We had our seniors, the Math Club team, and the juniors who had spent the previous week preparing for the competition.

If we're being completely honest, we knew our chances of sweeping trophies were slim. We were deep in the middle of our own internal assessments, and nobody expected a six-day crash course to turn us into Olympiad champions overnight.

The real plan? Step away from the campus grind, blow off some steam, enjoy a day together, and, of course, make the most of that coveted On-Duty (OD) attendance.

When we arrived, we were greeted by breakfast and a room full of colleges that had come ready to compete. Suddenly, our little training camp felt rather adventurous!

The symposium had a collection of events, each with its own flavour — relay-style problem solving, image puzzles, mathematical quizzes, and a treasure hunt that made you think on your feet.

We gave it our best, but the juniors had their fair share of match-day chaos. Last-minute team reshuffles, questions that went beyond what we'd covered, a few moments of hesitation, and the occasional arithmetic slip meant that things didn't always go our way.

And that's competition, I suppose. Sometimes you know the answer but don't submit in time. Sometimes you prepare for one thing and get tested on another. Sometimes one tiny calculation sends you down an entirely different path.

Meanwhile, our graduating seniors were playing in a league of their own.

They seemed to navigate the events on pure experience, cracking jokes between rounds and enjoying themselves far more than anyone worrying about a formula sheet probably should.

In Number Ninjas 2.0, seniors Dhilip and Vijayalakshmi made it all the way to the grand finals and secured an impressive 4th Place overall!

Of course, for our seniors, trophies were only a secondary bonus.

The real mission was St. Joseph's legendary hospitality: free, steaming-hot biryani and ice cream. 🍛🍨

While others were busy reviewing formulas between rounds, our gang was happily gathered around lunch, sharing plates, laughing over the morning's blunders, and enjoying the day.

Whenever someone got knocked out of a round, the seniors would simply shrug:

"We lost the battle, but the war is still on!"

And off they went.

The peak moment came when we all sat together for lunch. One of my seniors paused with his spoon in mid-air, pointed proudly down at his plate, and announced:

"Timothy, we lost the battle... but look at this. We have won the war!"

And honestly, with a mountain of biryani and free ice cream waiting, who was I to argue? 😂

We may not have walked away with a cabinet full of trophies, but we had pulled off something real. We took a group of first-years, built a training camp from scratch, travelled together, competed against some great colleges, and gave our seniors a memorable day.

For our first outing as a new team, that was a genuine victory.

05. More Than a Competition ✨

Looking back, I don't think the trophies were the only thing we were building.

We had organised a training programme with barely a week's notice. We had taught, practised, prepared, and brought a group of students together to represent our department.

My classmates and I learnt things we hadn't been taught in a classroom — how to prepare competition material, organise sessions, work with a team, and make things happen when time wasn't exactly on our side.

The seven juniors learnt, practised, and showed up despite everything else going on in college. Our seniors continued to guide us, just as they had promised when they recommended me for the presidency.

And somewhere in the middle of all that chaos, I began to understand what being President actually meant.

It wasn't about having all the answers.

It was about learning how to bring people together, taking responsibility when things got messy, asking for help when you needed it, and making sure the next person had an opportunity to try something they might not otherwise have tried.

The seniors had started something before me. They had trusted me to carry it forward, and I was slowly figuring out how to do that — not perfectly, but with the people around me.

To my seniors: thank you for trusting me and standing beside me.

To my classmates: thank you for jumping into unfamiliar work and helping make this happen.

And to the juniors who attended the training sessions, especially the seven who stayed: thank you for showing up and putting in the effort.

This was only the beginning.

We had more events to organise, more projects to build, more competitions to attend, and plenty more opportunities to learn the hard way.

But for our first attempt at putting together a proper Math Club training team?

Six days, seven juniors, twenty students at the competition, a whole lot of mathematics, and one victorious plate of biryani.

I'd say we did alright.

By God's grace, there's a lot more to come. ✝️
